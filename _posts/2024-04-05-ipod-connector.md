The first time I ever opened a classic iPod, I *immediately* broke it. I ended up having to send it away for repair because I applied too much pressure to the power connector while trying to remove the battery.  
  
The other day, I had to open up another classic iPod to attempt a repair. After slicing open my fingers and scratching up my hands while prying open the ridiculously difficult metal back, I encountered the dreaded power connector. It was 11 pm, raining outside, and I did not feel like swearing at the top of my lungs again because I had broken yet another tiny power connector.

Then, I remembered what happened last time I had my connector repaired. I received my iPod back with red goop all over the brand-new flashy power connector. I opened an email from the repairer, who told me that they had added glue to the connector as "it can be torn from the logic board too easily!" That got me thinking, maybe I should probably do the same! I hurried downstairs in a frantic search for super glue. I couldn't find any sitting around, but what I did find was a hot glue gun. I quickly searched on my phone to see if the stuff was conductive and came back with [probably not?](https://electronics.stackexchange.com/questions/48494/is-hot-glue-a-good-insulator)

Anyway, I raced back upstairs and mangled the insides of the iPod. The hard drive in this device was long gone, so I did my best to push it as far back as possible, out of the way of the surgical procedure.

Once the area was prepared, I put a tiny dab of glue right in front of the connector and let it sit for about 5 minutes. Here's how it looked:

![After Surgery](/assets/images/ipod1/PXL_20240404_120712293.jpg)

With the glue now dry, the moment of truth had arrived. Had I wasted all of my time? Was the original connector simply too weak, or had I managed to save this iPod from the soldering iron? Using pliers and a tiny spudger, I carefully pried up the connector tab. Finally, I let out a loud sigh of relief.
![Relief](/assets/images/ipod1/PXL_20240404_120837164.jpg)

Now I can remove the dead hard drive, and undo the connector for the headphone jack, hold switch and clicker.
![After Surgery](/assets/images/ipod1/PXL_20240404_121337620.jpg)

From here I removed the leftover rubber from the hard drive, cleaned up the inside a bit and then installed a new [iFlash Quad](https://www.iflash.xyz/store/iflash-quad/). I'm happy to say that iTunes did not freak out at all when reinstalling the OS and the iPod has been happy ever since!
![After Surgery](/assets/images/ipod1/PXL_20240404_124048723.jpg)

So yeah, if you've ever torn out that tiny little connector from a classic iPod before, maybe stop for a few minutes and reinforce it with some glue. It's going to save you a good $60 if you send it off to be repaired or if you can solder it yourself, less frustration with the iron in your hand!
Regardless, happy iPod modding everyone!
